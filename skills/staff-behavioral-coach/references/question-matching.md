# Question Matching

A strong story can fail when it proves an adjacent competency. Identify the evidence contract before selecting the example.

| Prompt family | Evidence that must appear | Common adjacent miss |
|---|---|---|
| Drive impact | Important problem, candidate agency, mobilized execution, defensible consequence | A large project with unclear personal causality |
| Embrace ambiguity | Candidate defined the problem, decision rights, investment thesis, and operating path | Executing well after leadership supplied direction |
| Influence without authority | Independent conviction, stakeholders outside reporting line, what each changed, joint result | Persuasive presentation with no changed decision |
| Align multiple teams | Distinct teams, competing constraints, what each team changed, shared outcome | One team coordinating with Product and Design |
| Resolve conflict | Real disagreement, other side's legitimate concern, candidate behavior, resolution, durable relationship | Proving the candidate was technically right |
| Rebuild trust | Damaged relationship, partner-directed repair, kept commitments, evidence confidence or behavior changed | Shipping successfully afterward |
| Significant failure | Candidate's mistake, concrete harm, direct accountability, repair, prevention mechanism, later proof of change | A difficult project that ultimately succeeded |
| Critical feedback | Specific feedback, why it was difficult, changed behavior, evidence the change worked | Disagreeing with feedback but defending the original approach |
| Complex technical problem | Symptom, investigation, evidence, root cause, consequential fix, validation | Architecture ownership without diagnosis |
| Innovation | New mechanism or reframing, prior alternatives, iteration, changed assumption, consequence | Sophisticated implementation with no novelty |
| Strategic leadership | Choice of problem, where not to invest, changed roadmap or allocation, durable direction | Strong execution against an assigned roadmap |
| Develop people | Diagnosis of growth need, tailored intervention, independent judgment or scope gained | Taking over the hard work or giving generic advice |
| Build organizational capability | Repeatable mechanism, adoption, independent behavior, organization-level leverage | One or two mentoring anecdotes |
| Data changed judgment | Initial belief, credible contradictory evidence, changed decision, better outcome | Data merely confirming the original plan |
| High-stakes incomplete decision | Missing information, downside, decision rule, reversible safeguards, validation | Recklessness described as speed |
| AI-driven impact | Work delegated to AI, human accountability, verification, iteration, attributable advantage, user or org consequence | Tool usage or generated-code volume |

## Selection rule

If a story cannot supply the required evidence without stretching facts, choose another story or mark a story-bank gap.

## Narrative hygiene

Keep a detail only if it strengthens question fit, judgment, influence, or impact. Move technically useful but nonessential material into follow-ups. Remove side projects and unresolved claims that attract the wrong interview thread.
